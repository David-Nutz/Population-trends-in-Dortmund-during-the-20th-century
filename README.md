# Population-trends-in-Dortmund-during-the-20th-century

This project examines the population development of Dortmund throughout the entire 20th century.

The analysis focuses not only on changes in the total population, but also on the underlying demographic processes:

- natural population development
- births and deaths
- migration into and out of Dortmund
- migration origins and destinations
- historical population fluctuations
- methodological differences between statistical sources
- the impact of economic, political and historical events on demographic development

A particular focus of the project is the comparison of data published by **Dortmund's municipal statistical office**, the **Statistical Office of North Rhine-Westphalia (IT.NRW)** and the **Statistisches Jahrbuch Deutscher Städte/Gemeinden**.

The project combines quantitative data analysis with historical source research in order to explain not only **what changed**, but also **why these changes occurred**.

## Objectives

The main objectives of the project were:

1. Reconstruct Dortmund's population development from **1900 to 2000**.
2. Extend the available digital population data by incorporating historical archival sources.
3. Analyse the contribution of **births, deaths and migration** to population change.
4. Identify major demographic phases throughout the 20th century.
5. Compare population figures from different statistical institutions.
6. Identify and investigate discrepancies between statistical sources.
7. Connect demographic developments with major historical and economic events.
8. Visualise long-term demographic trends in a clear and accessible way.

## Data Sources

1. Dortmund Statistics

Publications and historical documents from the municipal statistical office of Dortmund provide data on:

- population figures
- births
- deaths
- immigration
- emigration
- migration origins and destinations
- individual districts and municipalities

## IT.NRW

Data from the State Statistical Office of North Rhine-Westphalia were used to compare official population figures and demographic statistics with the municipal data.

## Statistisches Jahrbuch Deutscher Städte / Gemeinden

The statistical yearbooks provide an additional historical source.

An important methodological aspect is that the yearbooks were based on municipal data until the municipal boundary reform of **1 January 1975**. From 1975 onwards, the publications used data from the State Statistical Office.

## Stadtarchiv Dortmund

Historical documents from the **Dortmund City Archives** were used to investigate discrepancies, methodological changes and individual demographic developments that cannot be explained by the statistical datasets alone.

## Methodology
The project follows a combination of **exploratory data analysis, historical source analysis and comparative statistical analysis**.

Historical population, birth, death and migration data were collected from statistical publications and archival sources. Data from different statistical institutions were compared to identify discrepancies and methodological differences.

The statistical findings were then analysed in their historical context, including industrialisation, both World Wars, post-war reconstruction, the coal and steel crises, international migration and German reunification.

## Key findings

- Dortmund's population development throughout the 20th century was strongly shaped by industrialisation, migration, war, economic transformation and demographic change.

- The project reconstructed Dortmund's population development back to **1900**, considerably extending the previously available digital data.

- A central finding is that **different population figures for the same year or statistical subject were often caused by different statistical methods rather than simple errors**.

- For example, in **1956**, the population figure calculated by the Statistical Office of North Rhine-Westphalia differed from Dortmund's figure by **12,361 inhabitants**. This discrepancy was connected to different methods of recording migration and problems associated with the 1950 census.

- Other discrepancies resulted from different approaches to **second residences** and the statistical treatment of **refugee movements** after World War II.

- The source material was particularly rich for the **1950s and 1960s**, providing numerous documents that helped explain statistical discrepancies. For most later decades, comparable documentation was scarce. The reasons for this limited availability could not be conclusively established within the project.

- The analysis demonstrates that historical population statistics must be interpreted in their **methodological and historical context** rather than treated as directly comparable figures.

##Conclusion

The project demonstrates how **data analysis, historical source research and source criticism** can be combined to reconstruct long-term demographic trends and investigate **why different official statistics sometimes report different figures for the same population or period**.


## Future Work
